MY WORKFLOW / 01—08

My workflow, from planning to editing

From one idea to a finished episode: screenplay, character references, connected shots and subtitles. Choosing one convincing image and finishing an entire episode involve very different checks.

I did not know this workflow when I started. My first AI drama began as an action exercise. I struggled with character consistency and even with extending a scene to fifteen seconds; the early FALL 707 experiments had the same problems. This article pairs the tools I used with the materials and checks needed between stages.

01 / 08

First, decide what story to tell

The initial ideas were my own. FALL 707 began with a modern special forces soldier arriving in Joseon. The reboot developed that premise into a military fantasy occult thriller centered on a shaman antagonist and zombies.

Before moving on

Whose story is it? What do they want in this episode? What changes by the end? More scenes will not solve an unclear dramatic goal.

Handoff
One-line premise + episode goal

02 / 08

Develop the screenplay with Claude and GPT

I worked with Claude and GPT to develop my ideas into screenplays. The useful question is not simply how to add dialogue, but what each scene does. A line changes meaning depending on what the character knows and conceals.

Before moving on

Check time, place and forms of address against earlier episodes. Could an action convey the information instead? Any changed line also needs a sound and subtitle check later.

Handoff
A script with scene goals, actions and dialogue

03 / 08

Establish a visual character reference

I made the character sheets with GPT Image 2, defining costume, hair and presence as well as the face. Season 1’s black uniform and ponytail and Season 2’s camouflage and short bob belong to different seasonal references.

Before moving on

Compare front, side and full-body views within the same season. A generated patch, rank marking or piece of equipment is not automatically correct. A usable face does not settle costume continuity.

Handoff
Season-specific reference images + continuity notes

04 / 08

Break the scene into visible shots

Trying to generate everything in a scene at once can destabilize action, camera and space. Separate the shot that establishes the place, the shot that shows the action and the shot that holds the reaction. The actual film still below is for studying a shot, not a screenshot of the original storyboard or editing session.

Before moving on

Check positions, eyelines and movement into the next shot. Recording each shot’s starting and ending state makes hand and prop jumps easier to spot in the edit.

Handoff
Shot plan: framing, action, start and end states

05 / 08

Give the prompt roles and preservation criteria

I built prompt skills and used them as a writing framework. State whether a reference controls character appearance or spatial layout. Writing a reference tag and connecting the actual file in the generation interface are separate actions.

Before moving on

Check the event, camera position and motion, light direction, and the identity, costume and props to retain. Separate changes from preserved elements when revising.

Handoff
Shot requests linked to actual reference files

06 / 08

Generate short shots that can connect

I began with Seedance 2.0 and used Seedance 2.5 from Season 1, Episode 6. Early on, even a fifteen-second scene was difficult. A useful result must complete the needed action and leave a state the next shot can pick up.

Before moving on

Play the beginning, middle and end. Check identity, equipment, missing action and eyelines throughout. The early still below documents that experiment; it is not a benchmark of current models.

Handoff
Candidate clips + ranges needing revision

07 / 08

Record why a shot needs revision

In the early experiments, I did not get either the unit patches or the Joseon-period details right. I received a lot of criticism and gave up making FALL 707 for a time. I was still learning AI drama and character consistency. These are the images that remained in those films.

Before moving on

Record the time, the change, the elements to retain and the recheck. One attractive frame cannot settle continuity in movement and sound. The early-experiment note shows the actual patches in context.

Handoff
Revision reasons and recheck notes

08 / 08

Finish the edit, sound and subtitles

Generated shots still need to work as an episode. After assembling them, review dialogue boundaries, reaction beats and overlapping effects and music. For Season 3 onward, I decided to use SRT translation subtitles rather than burn translations into the picture.

Before moving on

Play the master with each SRT and check timing and line breaks. Review framing and title placement separately for the 16:9 episode and 9:16 short. Include the published player, subtitles and next-episode link in the final checklist.

Handoff
Final master, language SRTs and release copy
